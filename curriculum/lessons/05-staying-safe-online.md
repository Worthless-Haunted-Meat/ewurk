# Staying safe online

EWURK topic: **Lesson 5 — Staying safe online**

Instructor: read each numbered step aloud. Attendance is marked in EWURK after class—not on this page.

## Goal

Everyone can name three ways to stay safer online (strong passwords, not sharing private info, asking a trusted adult) and spot one “too good to be true” message.

## Materials

- EWURK lab Linux desktop with browser
- Whiteboard for “OK to share” vs “keep private” lists (no real student data on the board)

## Steps

1. Draw two columns: **OK to share online** (favorite color, hobby) and **Keep private** (home address, school name, passwords, photos you did not want public). Fill together—use only fictional examples.
2. Explain passwords like house keys: not on sticky notes, not told to friends. Show how to make a **passphrase** (four random words) and type it once in a blank text file, then delete the file—do not save real passwords in class.
3. In the browser, open a site your program already uses (library, school district, or Wikipedia). Point out the **lock** icon or `https` in the address bar when the instructor’s connection supports it; say “We only type passwords on sites a trusted adult chose.”
4. Read aloud a short fictional scam message you wrote on paper: “You won a free phone! Click here!” Ask: “What feels wrong?” (urgency, free prize, asks for personal info). Do **not** click unknown links as a demo.
5. End with the rule: **When unsure, pause and ask a parent, guardian, or EWURK staff member before typing or clicking.**

## If the room is chaotic (10 minutes)

Skip the browser. Only do the two-column poster on paper and practice the passphrase rule with silly words (e.g. `purple-turtle-happy-cloud`) spoken not written down.

## Done looks like

- Learners can name at least one item from each column (share vs private).
- Everyone repeats the “ask a trusted adult” rule once.
- No real passwords or family details collected or stored on lab machines.
